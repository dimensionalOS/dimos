# M20 in the GTA/Sim2 experiment

This is on `pim/feat/experimental-gta-sim2-g1`, based on Pim's Sim2 messages,
USD/HardwareModule composition, physics and sensor stack. It does not use the
older M20 simulation as its dynamics engine. G1 remains a separate session.

## Integration

`prepare_m20` replaces only the robot in a copy of the authored street patch.
Native MJCF goes through MuJoCo's USD importer, with explicit repair of visual
collision flags and material bindings. An audit compares collision counts,
primitive sizes/transforms, friction, contact solver parameters, mass/inertia,
joint dynamics and motor limits to native MJCF. HardwareModule mounts contribute an IMU and
camera. Encoders and ideal attitude observations are acquired on the simulation
clock, joined by acquisition time and consumed once per 20 ms boundary.

The actor publishes the current Sim2 atomic five-interface `ActionCommand`:
position, velocity, kp, kd and effort, with clock ID, sequence and 100 ms expiry.
Leg joints have position targets; wheels have velocity targets with zero kp.
Releasing controls selects a physical parking stance: legs hold home angles,
wheels hold their measured angles at the stop transition (kp 20, kd 2), and
actor history resets. This reduces the public actor's observed zero-command
creep; it is not a world-position lock. Movement selects the actor again with its original gains. Out-of-range
actor position targets are bounded for the message contract, with the equivalent
PD torque retained as feedforward effort.

Motor limits remain 76.4 Nm for legs and 21.6 Nm for SDK wheels (20 Nm for
DreamWaQ wheels). No force multiplier, root-velocity override or teleport drives
locomotion. The blueprint rejects a scene with the wrong home pose/gains.

The SixNG observer renders measured articulated body poses independently of
physics. Sim2's camera uses its own acquisition contract. The browser image is
an observer stream, not a fabricated Sim2 camera publication. No lidar is mounted
in this first M20 configuration.

## Reproduce

Extract the SDK archive to an asset directory, then supply the generated road
patch and an output directory on the secondary SSD:

```bash
python -m dimos.experimental.gta_sim2.prepare_m20 \
  --patch /path/to/patch-navroad-hd \
  --model dimos/experimental/gta_sim2/assets/m20/m20.xml \
  --meshes /path/to/m20_sdk/meshes --output /path/to/patch-m20-road --heading-degrees 90

python -m dimos.experimental.gta_sim2.demo_m20_model_audit \
  --model dimos/experimental/gta_sim2/assets/m20/m20.xml \
  --meshes /path/to/m20_sdk/meshes --scene /path/to/patch-m20-road/world.usda

MUJOCO_GL=egl python -m dimos.experimental.gta_sim2.demo_m20_eval \
  --scene /path/to/patch-m20-road/world.usda --policy /path/to/m20_sdk/policy.onnx \
  --terrain flat --speed 1.5 --seconds 6 --output /path/to/result.json
```

Use `--terrain stairs --speed 1 --seconds 12` for five synthetic 10 cm risers,
35 cm treads and a landing. These fixtures are evaluation-only and never replace
the interactive map's authored collision geometry. Use `--profile dreamwaq342`
for both preparation and evaluation with the ArenaX checkpoint.
`--step-height .15 --record` records the 15 cm fixture, composed USD and measured
WorldStates. The evaluation stops after the robot reaches x=4 m and remains on
the landing for two seconds. `demo_m20_replay` renders that recording without
stepping or modifying dynamics:

```bash
MUJOCO_GL=egl python -m dimos.experimental.gta_sim2.demo_m20_replay \
  --scene /path/to/result.composed.usda --recording /path/to/result.worldstates \
  --output /path/to/stairs.mp4
```

`demo_interactive --robot m20 --policy ...` uses the same gateway/render transport
as G1, with separate runtime directory, camera port and HTTP service. Runtime
assets and experiment reports remain on the secondary SSD, outside Git.

## Measured limitations (2026-11-26)

The initial comparison was invalidated by a conversion defect: MuJoCo 4.0's
MJCF exporter left visual meshes collidable on reimport, adding 17 unintended
colliders, and omitted the native friction material. Explicit
`physics:collisionEnabled=false` fixes the visual geoms. Contact friction must
also be bound as the all-purpose material: this decoder ignores a physics-only
binding when a visual material is already bound. A regression test covers both.

After those fixes, full ModuleCoordinator evaluations measured:

| Trial | ArenaX result |
|---|---|
| Flat, 1.5 m/s command, 6 seconds | 1.42 m/s along initial heading; minimum upright cosine 0.989 |
| Authored road, 1 m/s command, 4 seconds | 0.90 m/s; minimum upright cosine 0.997 |
| Five 10 cm risers, 35 cm treads | Reached landing and parked; minimum upright cosine 0.954 |
| Five 15 cm risers, 35 cm treads | Reached landing and parked, repeated with recorded body poses; minimum upright cosine 0.928 |
| Five 20 cm risers, 35 cm treads | Reached landing and parked; minimum upright cosine 0.884 |

The SDK baseline still stalls at the first 10 cm step, despite averaging
1.48 m/s in the corrected flat trial. Its parked road displacement was 8.6 mm
over eight seconds. The stair results are short synthetic trials, not a success
rate across randomized terrain: descent, narrow stairs, damaged treads, payloads
and real hardware are unvalidated. ArenaX drifts sideways about 0.7 m by the
landing in the open-loop stair tests. No heading controller or planner is added.

Physics still covers the existing 16 × 16 m patch, with commands stopped at the
inner boundary. This does not yet implement whole-world collision streaming,
physics LOD handoff, doors or movable props. Driving into the map's visible
scenery outside the authored collision coverage remains unsupported.

## Browser validation

Desktop input with ArenaX drove 2.9 m along the road and remained upright. Follow,
robot and free cameras, stop and full deployment reset passed. Mobile touch/release
passed with H.264 NVENC and 22.2 decoded fps over five seconds while the other
viewers remained running. A 20-second parked road trial displaced 16 mm and stayed upright. Input expiry, disconnect stop, exclusive ownership and origin
checks passed. Unit coverage includes profile order/history/reset, wheel position
versus velocity interfaces, bounded-target torque equivalence, parking latch,
clock/expiry and duplicate acquisitions (18 experiment tests total).

The observer composes hardware in blueprint order, as physics does. Different
layer orders can produce different scene revisions even with matching body IDs;
the renderer retains the exact revision check and reports mismatches explicitly.
